How the data is collected

A collector snapshots Gametime’s (unofficial) mobile API on a grid anchored to each game’s start time: daily from when a game appears on the schedule, every 6 hours inside a week, hourly inside 24 hours, and every 15 minutes from T-6h to T+30m. Every Kraken game gets the full treatment; every other NHL game enters at listing level 8 hours before its start. Each snapshot records every listing’s section, row, quantity options, price before fees, all-in price, and Gametime’s internal deal_score.

Three honest limitations. (1) These are asks, not transactions: a vanished listing was sold or delisted, we cannot distinguish. (2) Prices are the API’s base all-in prices; Gametime’s apps apply a display layer of roughly 0-10% on top, varying by listing. (3) Floors exclude junk listings via a deal_score > 1.5 filter (broker placeholders at 10-30x fair value).

The deal score, decomposed

Empirically, deal_score = all-in price ÷ V where V is Gametime’s modeled fair value for that exact seat at that event (verifiable: price/score is constant across listings in the same row to within rounding). V is recoverable per listing, which gives the dataset the platform’s own seat-quality model for free. Two caveats discovered along the way: V inflates sharply inside the final hours (so scores are not comparable across time near puck drop), and V underweights row depth relative to human preference, meaning front-row-adjacent bargains are systematically under-badged by the app.
